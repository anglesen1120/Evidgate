## 1. What this document is, and what it is not

It is the **criteria document** of 10.8.1-T1: the six selection criteria with their weights, the scoring scale, the disqualifier rules, and a shortlist of five candidate hosting options scored on the evidence that exists today.

It is **not** the decision memo. Specifically, it does **not**:

- choose a vendor, rank a winner, or start procurement (owner decision O3; area plan 10 §11);
- contain measured numbers — restore time, presign parity and ingest latency are measured by 10.8.1-T2 and recorded in the spike log (AC2); cost arithmetic at the pilot profile is 10.8.1-T3's (AC3);
- assert any external fact without a source link (§10). Numbers that are published list prices are marked as such; everything a vendor does not publish is marked ⚠️ `[OPEN]` with the spike step that closes it.

The scores in §5 describe **documented evidence today**, not the providers. Two of the six criteria (managed PostgreSQL 18 + PITR, CI-region latency) cannot be scored from documents at all; their scores are provisional and are replaced by the T2 measurements.

## 3. Scoring scale and gates

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 3 | Documented, published by the provider, no caveat |
| 2 | Documented with a caveat (subset API, published price higher, a documented service limit) or ⚠️ secondary source only |
| 1 | Not documented anywhere the team can cite: the spike must establish it before any recommendation; **1 is not a pass** |
| 0 | Documented as absent or disqualified |

Rules:

1. A **gate** criterion (C1, C2) at 0 disqualifies the candidate; it is recorded in the T3 memo with its reason, never dropped from the table (area plan 10 §5/§8).
2. A weighted total is **indicative only**. C1 is a policy gate, not a tradeable score: a cross-border candidate (score 1) may only be chosen together with the PDPL Art. 20 transfer assessment owned by plan 11. The owner, not this document, decides.
3. Provisional scores (C2 for the Vietnamese providers, C5 for everyone) are marked `⚠️ provisional` and are replaced by T2 measurements before T3 writes verdicts.
4. Every score must be traceable to a row of §4 or §6, which carries the source. T60: no fabricated metrics.

## 6. Criterion notes and disqualifier logic

### 6.1 C1 — residency (gate)

- VN-region options (A–C) keep artifact and metadata storage in Vietnam: no PDPL Art. 20 cross-border transfer, no transfer-impact dossier, no Art. 8.4 revenue-based fine exposure for the pilot data (DECISIONS F7/F8 ✅).
- APAC options (D–E) are **not** disqualified, but they move the transfer assessment and the 60-day filing into the critical path (DECISIONS F8; plan 11). RECOMMENDATIONS O3 already states this fallback, so both arms stay on the shortlist.
- Region count matters for the restore drill: an in-VN provider with one region still passes §4's drill requirement (restore into a clean project, area plan 10 §4 step 4), but a single-region provider gives no cross-region DR story — record it, do not silently score it away `[INFERENCE]`.

### 6.2 C2 — managed PostgreSQL 18 with PITR (gate)

- The gate is per area plan 10 §3 + §5/§8: no PITR ⇒ disqualifier; no managed PostgreSQL 18 ⇒ disqualifier (00 §11 pins 18; DECISIONS §4 records PostgreSQL 18 as supported until 2030-11-14, source S21).
- Evidence required from each shortlisted candidate at T2: the engine version actually provisioned (`SHOW server_version`), and a **timed restore to a point in time**, recorded in the spike log. The managed-PITR *capability* claim for D is documented and fine-grained (transaction logs to S3 every five minutes, S3), which is why D scores 3 while A–C score 1: A–C document backups and restore points, not WAL-based PITR.
- Note for the T3 residency note: the DECISIONS §4 PostgreSQL 18 support window and the 06-O7 retention decision (30 days, backups included) bound the PITR retention that must be configurable (10.5.1-T1) ⚠️ `[OPEN]` for A–C, whose maximum retention is not published.

### 6.3 C3 — S3-compatible object storage

- The product uses the S3 API only (DECISIONS §4); presigned URLs are bearer credentials with an expiry (DECISIONS §4 source ✅). Presign parity is therefore a **behaviour** criterion, not a branding one: T18 requires each candidate to be measured with a signed `Content-Length` plus checksum header in T2 (10.8.1-T2.2/T2.4).
- Compatible-subset risk: GCS's XML API is interoperable with S3 tools and supports V4 signed headers and HMAC keys (S7 ✅), but a subset is still a subset — E scores 2, not 3. A–C score 2 for the same reason (S3-compatibility claims, parity unmeasured).

### 6.4 C4 — egress cost

- Scored on **published list price availability and tier level**, because the pilot's egress volume is 10.9.1-T2's and 10.8.1-T3's arithmetic, not this document's (T60).
- D and E publish self-serve internet egress tiers (S4, S8, and Cloud SQL's own storage-and-networking section S23 — ⚠️ tiers not read in this session; the T3 memo must snapshot the page and date it, and must use the Cloud SQL network-egress line for E rather than the VM tier). A–C publish compute tiers but not egress prices ⚠️ `[OPEN]`; a quote is a sales step and an unknown cost, hence score 1 — **not** a statement that Vietnamese egress is expensive. Domestic/in-region traffic between the database and object storage inside one provider is normally not charged `[INFERENCE]`, which is a reason in-VN pairs may turn out cheaper; the spike should record the traffic path.

### 6.5 C5 — CI-region latency (unscoreable from documents)

- GitHub-hosted standard runners run on Azure (Linux/Windows) and on GitHub's own macOS infrastructure, and the region is **not selectable** for standard runners ✅ S19; only larger runners with Azure private networking are pinned to a customer-chosen Azure region ✅ S20. So "the pilot's CI region" is not a documentable constant: latency must be **measured** from a real workflow run for each candidate, which is exactly what 10.8.1-T2.2/T2.4 already specify. The probe should cover more than one APAC region per hyperscaler where the provider offers one nearer to Vietnam (GCP Bangkok `asia-southeast3` ✅ S23; AWS Malaysia `ap-southeast-5` / Thailand `ap-southeast-7` ✅ S24), because the region choice is a latency decision, not a residency one.
- Every candidate therefore scores the same provisional 1. When T2 lands the numbers, C5 differentiates.

### 6.6 C6 — operational burden for one team

- VN providers (A–C) keep the team inside one portal and one invoice and remove the cross-border paperwork; they cost documentation and console maturity. A's documented service limits (no database proxy, no single→cluster conversion, one backup/restore job per cluster) are real operational constraints and are the reason A is 2 rather than 3 despite 24/7 support ✅ S11, S12.
- The hyperscalers (D, E) score 3 on console/API/IaC maturity and on running both the database and the object storage with one IAM plane, but the burden returns as the PDPL dossier (plan 11) and the on-call runbooks for a foreign region.
- `[OPEN]` support SLA and data-processing agreement (DPA) terms are not published for A–C at the level plan 11 needs (Art. 37.2.a requires a processing contract, DECISIONS F8); T2/T3 must obtain them.
