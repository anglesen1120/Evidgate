## 1. Goal and non-goals

**Goal.** Produce an owner-facing decision memo for O3 (cloud provider/region) that compares at least two candidate providers on residency, managed PostgreSQL 18 with PITR, S3-compatible object storage, egress cost, latency from the pilot's CI region, and operational burden for one team — with measured (not brochure) numbers from a hands-on spike, so the owner can pick a provider without further research. No vendor is selected and no procurement happens inside this plan.

**Non-goals.** Choosing the vendor (owner decision O3); provisioning the final staging/prod environments (10.1.1-T3); PDPL/DPA filings and cross-border transfer assessment (11, informed by the memo's residency note); negotiated pricing (the memo uses list price + measured usage, not a sales quote).

## 2. User stories and acceptance criteria

- As the owner, I read one memo and can pick a provider, because each candidate's restore time, latency and cost figures are measured, and disqualifiers are stated plainly.
- As the Tech Lead, a candidate that cannot meet PITR or residency is recorded as a disqualifier, not silently dropped from the comparison.

| # | Acceptance criterion (testable) |
|---|---|
| AC1 | A criteria document exists scoring residency options (VN/APAC), managed PostgreSQL 18 with PITR, S3-compatible storage, egress cost, CI-region latency and single-team operational burden; at least two candidate providers are shortlisted |
| AC2 | A hands-on spike provisions managed PostgreSQL 18 on each shortlisted candidate and records: measured PITR restore time, presigned-URL behaviour on its S3-compatible storage (signed `Content-Length` + checksum header parity, T18), and ingest latency from a CI-like runner — numbers committed to the spike log |
| AC3 | `docs/decisions/` carries a decision memo: comparison table with the measured numbers, per-candidate disqualifiers, a list-price cost estimate at the pilot profile, and a residency note addressed to 11.8.1; it explicitly chooses no vendor |
| AC4 | The memo's residency section is sufficient for 11.8.1 to open its PDPL/residency filing options without re-running the spike |

## 3. Contracts touched

- DECISIONS §8 O3 — this story produces the memo that feeds the owner's decision; the decision itself is recorded in DECISIONS §10 afterward, outside this story.
- 00 §11 RPO/RTO (15 min / 4 h) — PITR capability and measured restore time are scored against it.
- T18 — presign parity is measured on each candidate, not assumed.
- T60 — every number in the memo is measured in the spike or is a published list price; no figure is fabricated.
- No schema, permission or API change.

## 4. Data model and migrations

None. Deliverables are documents and throwaway spike environments; all spike resources are torn down after measurement.

## 5. API shapes (commands, queries, endpoints)

None. No product endpoint is involved; spike scripts call provider APIs directly (provider SDKs/CLI in `scripts/spikes/provider-eval/`).

## 6. States and transitions

None. The memo records a `recommended | acceptable | disqualified` verdict per candidate — a document classification, not a stored state machine.

## 7. Jobs and integrations

- Spike stands up and tears down managed PostgreSQL 18 + object storage on two candidates, driven by scripts in `scripts/spikes/provider-eval/` so the measurement is repeatable if a third candidate is added.
- Output feeds 10.1.1-T3 (final environment bootstrap targets), 10.5.1-T1 (backup/PITR configuration uses the chosen provider's feature), 10.6.1-T2 (real-provider presign smoke) and 11.8.1 (residency/PDPL filing options).
- The spike must not touch staging or any environment holding real data; a dedicated throwaway project/account per candidate is used (area-plan §11 recommends separate accounts for staging/prod — the spike is its own account).
