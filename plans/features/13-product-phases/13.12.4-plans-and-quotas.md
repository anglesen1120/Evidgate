## 1. Goal and non-goals

**P1 · G2.** An authorized staff operator can publish a subscription plan, assign a published revision to one named organization, and see what its next metered request can consume. A switch changes the allowance, not the usage already recorded. Hosted minutes are included monthly: exhaustion refuses new work; it does not generate an overage charge (OI-13-806).

**Non-goals.** No price editor, payment collection, backdated assignment, pro-rata invoice, change to historical statements, customer self-service, or new credit ledger. Hosted job isolation, concurrency and image settings are not subscription quotas. Publishing or assigning a plan is not authority to transfer money or credit.

## 2. User stories and acceptance criteria

| ID | Actor and action | Observable outcome |
|---|---|---|
| AC1 | A provisioned operator with billing authority publishes a revised plan. | A new immutable revision is available for assignment immediately; its predecessor remains readable. Two requests based on the same revision cannot both publish. Invalid limits or a failed audit leave the old revision active. |
| AC2 | An authorized operator assigns a published revision to org A mid-month. | The first metering decision after commit uses the new limit against A's existing period usage and outstanding reservations. No other org changes. A new catalog revision does not silently move A off its assigned revision. |
| AC3 | Two new requests compete for the last included unit. | At most one gets it. A request that exactly fits can proceed; a request exceeding headroom is refused with no new job or overage. An ingest retry and a hosted settlement replay still count once. |
| AC4 | The operator lowers a quota below current use, then raises it. | Current use may exceed the new limit. No refund, correction, debt or overage is created; new work is blocked until it fits. Raising the limit restores only the difference, not a fresh allowance. Prior-period statements and existing entries are unchanged. |
| AC5 | The operator changes an AI allowance or requests an AI grant/adjustment. | A plan change never posts a credit entry. A grant/adjustment goes through 08.5.5-T5; above threshold it cannot post without a different authorized approver. Zero available credit or insufficient plan headroom prevents a new provider call. |
| AC6 | A user attempts a tenant write without the target grant, using only billing authority, or from the public host. | No tenant state or effective assignment is disclosed and no mutation occurs. The authorized console distinguishes stale, refused, unavailable and genuinely exhausted states. |

## 3. Contracts touched

- FR-13-14 and 13.12.1-T2/T3 supply the staff-only ingress, authenticated session, immutable revision history, `ETag`/`If-Match` and atomic audited publication. FR-02-13 and 02.8.1-T2 govern every tenant-specific read or write, not the global plan catalog.
- 12.3.1-T4 owns `IQuotaGate.CheckAsync`, scope order, UTC periods, warning/refusal and atomic reservation. 04.8.1-T1 owns idempotent ingest metering (`results`, not the wire name `event`); 13.11.1-T11 owns hosted reservation and once-only settlement of `runner_minutes`. Neither emitter gets a second quota service.
- 08.5.5 owns the AI credit account, bigint micro-units, reserve/settle/refund, caps and `ai_credit_exhausted`. A plan's included AI amount is a period spending ceiling; spendable balance still comes from the guarded credit operation. Increasing a ceiling does not top up an account. Existing opt-in, kill switch and stricter caps continue to apply.
- Only allow supported metric/unit pairs with an actual enforcement path, such as the result count per accepted batch. Preserve the registered unit and precision; `ai_tokens` is not fungible with `ai_credit`. Never offer an unmetered metric as an enforced allowance, or display an unavailable measurement as zero.

## 4. Data model and migrations

| Record | Required invariant |
|---|---|
| Global plan identity and immutable revisions | Stable plan ID and revision ID; validated limits keyed by canonical metric, unit and period, one value per key. Required metered limits are explicit, finite and nonnegative, representable in their native units; zero means no new use. No missing-value-as-unlimited fallback. Publish through the shared revision store; no credential fields. |
| Org assignment revision | The record binds the explicit org to an exact published plan revision and names its predecessor. Server UTC activation time, not a client-supplied backdate. At most one current assignment per org; use an org-scoped index/current pointer and FORCE RLS on tenant rows. Prior assignments, including their target revision IDs, remain readable under the original scope. |
| Existing meters and audit | Reuse `quota_counter`, the usage ledger and AI account/entries. Keep the current period and all dedupe keys; do not key usage by assignment or by plan version. Append audit metadata and switch the active pointer in the same transaction. Include actor, UTC time, request ID, before/after non-secret values or revision refs, canonical redacted hashes and the tenant grant ID where applicable. |

Separate plan allowance from the existing monotone stored quota limit: migration 0079 prevents lowering `quota_limit` or `used`. Do not weaken that guard or implement a downgrade with `RaiseQuotaLimit`. Resolve the active plan ceiling in the atomic gate decision, applying any independently configured, stricter tenant/project cap as well. For an assigned metric, a missing current-period counter must not take the legacy no-counter/allow branch. Initialize once with trustworthy period usage; if usage or live reservations cannot be reconciled, refuse admission until they can. A new period starts only at the meter's normal UTC boundary.

## 5. API shapes (commands, queries, endpoints)

All new operations are under `/internal/operator/*` on the operator ingress, never a public `/v1` alias. Perform auth before returning catalog, tenant, history or mutation details. Global catalog operations need no tenant impersonation. Use the shared shell's antiforgery and revision preconditions.

| Operation | Input and observable result |
|---|---|
| List/read plans; publish plan revision | Read plan IDs, labels, revisions and typed allowances; publish new values against the expected catalog/plan revision. Return the committed revision and ETag. On stale `If-Match`, return 412 with the authorized current revision, never silently overwrite. |
| Read/replace current org assignment | Scoped org path, expected assignment revision (shared initial-revision convention when unassigned), exact published plan revision ID and idempotency key. Return effective UTC time, old/new revision references and authorized period headroom. Reject unknown org, invalid target, backdating or missing grant without changing either tenant. |
| Grant/adjust AI credit | Use the existing `POST /internal/operator/orgs/{o}/ai-credit` and its `billing:write` command, not a plan-editor balance field. Bind the idempotent intent and revision precondition to the account/org; the amount, reason and applicable approval belong to 08.5.5-T5. Validate any approval against the same intent and a different authorized identity. Report posted only after the domain entry commits. |

Check authorization on every replay. Same operation/key/target and payload returns the original successful outcome, not a fresh assignment or credit; a changed payload under the same key conflicts. A distinct stale edit remains a 412. Invalid or denied operations never appear as successful revisions.

## 6. States and transitions

1. **Published → assigned.** Publishing selects the current revision for the catalog; assigning a revision changes only the chosen org. The effective point is commit. Concurrent assignments and admissions must serialize so a single request never reserves under two revisions or sees a partly activated revision. No redeploy or eventual background sync is required for correctness.
2. **Assigned → switched within a period.** For a positive request, compare against the new limit less settled consumption and outstanding commitments, counting each commitment once. Example fixture, not product pricing: 70 minutes settled and 10 reserved; switching to 100 leaves 20 available, switching to 60 leaves zero. Preserve the existing reservation for normal settlement/refund, even after downgrade. A switch back to 100 restores 20 if nothing else changed. No proration or rewind.
3. **Exhausted → available.** A higher ceiling, released reservation or the meter's normal period rollover may restore headroom. Rollover uses the current assignment and new period, not the previous plan's limit. Pin existing work to its reservation period; settling across midnight cannot spend twice or give next month's balance an extra refund. Resetting AI period spend never mints a credit grant.
4. **Credit requested → posted or refused.** For an amount above the existing threshold, missing or self-approval posts nothing. With only one operator this change remains unapplied. Exactly at the threshold use the ordinary 08.5.5-T5 rule; do not add a second approval to routine plan publication or assignment.

## 7. Jobs and integrations

- At admission, resolve the committed assignment and exact plan revision by indexed lookup in the existing gate's tenant transaction. Enforce both tenant and project quota without incrementing one on refusal by the other. Use the same effective limit for warning, refusal/stamping and authorized summary; preserve `quota_exceeded` and `Retry-After` behavior.
- Hosted admission reserves before creating runnable work and settles actual usage once, including interruption; unused reservation is released under the original key. Do not convert exhaustion into a paid run. AI reservation checks the plan ceiling, spent and already-reserved credit as well as available balance and existing caps **before** contacting the provider; completion still settles/refunds the existing reservation.
- Do not run a repricing, ledger-rewrite or monthly credit top-up job on publish or assignment. Existing metering emitters, period rollover and rollups remain responsible for actual consumption. A delayed notification cannot postpone activation or authorize a stale admission.

## 8. Security, privacy and performance

- **Authorization:** operator host, real workforce MFA and provisioned `platform_operator` session; preserve `billing:write` for mutations and the existing billing read permission for staff usage/history. Require active, target-matching, tenant-admin-approved support scope for tenant actions and audit scoped reads with grant ID. A revoked/expired grant, other tenant's grant, customer admin, CI token or billing role alone cannot authorize access. Threshold approval is additional, never a substitute for these checks.
- **Retention/deletion:** plan and assignment revisions are append-only. Retain accounting entries and their audit under the existing Usage, AI accounting and audit policies; tenant deletion follows the existing authorized deletion/anonymization workflow. Neither switch nor rollback erases an earlier assignment, a usage row or a receipt.
- **Telemetry:** bounded outcomes for published, assigned, stale, denied and exhausted by registered metric; record org and grant only in authorized audit. No request bodies, tenant payload, credentials, secret-derived hashes or secret values in UI/API/history/logs/traces. This story has no secret input; credential writes elsewhere stay write-only via the secret store.
- **Failure path:** an invalid limit, stale revision, unavailable authoritative state or failed audit never activates partial configuration or enlarges headroom. Keep the last valid persisted revision; if the gate cannot establish the current assignment and usage, refuse new metered work rather than assume unlimited capacity. No job/provider call on refusal. Existing accepted work may settle normally.

## 11. Rollout

1. Load cost-backed test/pilot plan revisions only after the corresponding owner limits are known. Existing tenants keep their existing gates until explicitly assigned; do not synthesize an unlimited default for an enrolled org. For first enrollment, reconcile existing period counters, reservations and ledger dedupe identities before activating the assignment.
2. Assign a pilot org on the operator ingress. Demonstrate a same-period downgrade, recovery, month rollover, exhausted hosted/AI refusal and exact-once settlement on the real consuming paths. Confirm both the assignment and its audit become visible without redeploy.
3. Roll back by publishing/assigning a new audited revision referring to the last known-good settings; never delete history, reverse a completed settlement or restore an old balance snapshot. A credit correction, if actually warranted, is a separate 08.5.5-T5 command.
